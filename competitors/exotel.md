# Exotel — competitor profile

Researched 2026-10-28 from exotel.com (pricing hub, AI voice agent product page,
Gen-AI voice agents page). AI per-minute pricing is **not public**.

**One-liner:** "Human-like conversations. Real business outcomes."
**Type:** Legacy Indian CPaaS that has bolted AI agents onto its contact-centre
stack.
**Threat level:** **Medium-high on enterprise budget, low on self-serve.**
They win the RFP; they cannot win a principal who wants to start today.

---

## 1. What they sell

Cloud telephony and contact-centre infrastructure — numbers, voice/SMS APIs,
IVR, agent desktop, call quality — plus an AI layer on top: AI voice agents, AI
chat agents, conversation quality analysis, and an MCP server for agentic
integrations. Their platform brand is **Harmony**.

Scale claim: **25B+ interactions per year.** This is a serious, established
vendor with enterprise references, not a startup.

## 2. Feature set

| Area | What they ship |
|---|---|
| Telephony | Indian virtual numbers, voice API, voice streaming, SMS |
| Contact centre | Inbound queues, agent desktop, supervisor tools, IVR flows |
| AI voice agents | Human-like outbound/inbound conversations over the same numbers |
| AI chat agents | WhatsApp/web chat equivalents |
| Quality analysis | Conversation quality analysis — automated call scoring |
| Developer surface | Voice API, streaming, **MCP server** for tool integrations |
| Platform | Harmony — the umbrella console for numbers, flows, users |
| Analytics | Dashboards, call records, recordings, quality scores |

## 3. User flow (as a new customer sees it)

1. Land on exotel.com → almost every path ends at **"Talk to an Expert"**.
2. Sales qualification: volumes, use case, existing CRM, number requirements.
3. Contract + KYC/DLT registration for numbers.
4. Onboarding with an account manager; flows built in Harmony, sometimes with
   Exotel professional services.
5. Go live; AI agents configured on top of the existing telephony estate.

There is **no self-serve price list and no card checkout** for the AI product.

## 4. Pricing

| Item | Published? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| AI voice agent per minute | **No** | quote only, sales-led |
| Voice API / telephony | Partial | number rental + usage, varies by plan |
| Contact centre seats | Quote | seat-based enterprise contracts |
| Free trial | Demo-led | "book a demo", not credits |

Publicly you can find telephony-style rates, but **the number a buyer actually
wants — cost per AI minute — is not on the site.** That is the gap.

## 5. The words they use

Headlines: *"Human-like conversations. Real business outcomes."*,
*"AI voice agents"*, *"Gen-AI voice agents"*, *"conversation quality analysis"*,
*"Harmony"*, *"Talk to an Expert"*.

Keyword vocabulary: enterprise, scale, reliability, compliance, DLT, contact
centre, omnichannel, collections, customer experience, outcomes, integrations,
MCP.

Use cases they name explicitly: **EMI reminders and collections, appointment
coordination, lead re-engagement** — i.e. they are already selling the
outbound-calling job. Tone is enterprise-conservative: trust, uptime, security,
case studies, logos.

## 6. Where they are stronger

- **Enterprise trust and references.** 25B interactions/year, large logos,
  security posture a procurement team recognises.
- **Full contact centre**, including inbound queues and human agent desktop —
  we have no inbound product.
- **Numbers and DLT compliance** handled in-house at scale.
- **Services arm** — they will build the campaign for you (for a fee).
- **Quality analysis** — automated scoring of conversations is a real feature we
  do not have.
- **MCP server** — a credible developer-integration story.

## 7. Gaps we attack

1. **Price opacity.** A principal cannot learn the cost of one campaign without
   a sales call. We publish ₹3/min and show the wallet live.
2. **Time to value.** Their path is demo → contract → KYC → onboarding. Ours is
   sign up → import → launch, same day.
3. **Seat-based enterprise contracts** with committed volumes. A 200-student
   attendance pilot does not fit their deal shape; it fits ours exactly.
4. **Builder, not workflow.** Same as everyone: you get agent infrastructure, not
   a college attendance product with roster, verification and report.
5. **Services dependency.** Custom builds mean the customer cannot self-serve
   changes; we expose prompts, escalation rules and templates in the UI.
6. **No transparent concurrency control** — capacity is an enterprise
   conversation, not a gauge on a dashboard.

## 8. Demo moves / how we win side-by-side

- Lead with **"what does one campaign cost?"** — we answer in one screen; they
  answer with a meeting.
- Show **time-to-first-call**: minutes from signup, not weeks from contract.
- Offer a **pilot-sized deal** (₹2,990 Starter) where their minimum contract
  does not fit.
- Concede inbound and contact-centre depth. Position as the **outbound campaign
  layer** that can coexist with their telephony later.
- If the buyer is an enterprise with an existing Exotel estate, sell the
  **vertical campaign + report** as an overlay rather than a replacement.
